# Three-minute presenter script

**0:00–0:30 — Set the scene**
Open the public control room and show the permanent `DEMO01` QR code. Ask participants to scan it and choose issuer, fund, and bank roles. If a device needs to switch people, choose **Kick**; that person can scan again or press **Rejoin**.

"Harbor Dollar is a fictional issuer. It has $2bn in obligations, $300m in bank cash, and $1.7bn in assumed eligible fund holdings. Customers request $450m in bank payouts. We need $50m left after payment, so the issuer requests $200m from the fund."

**0:30–1:15 — Request and process**
"Issuer treasury requests cash. Fund operations accepts the request. Notice that bank cash remains $300m. The fund then processes the redemption: fund holdings fall to $1.5bn and $200m becomes pending proceeds. Pending means the bank has not confirmed receipt."

**1:15–1:55 — Confirm and pay**
"Bank operations confirms the incoming $200m. Available cash now reaches $500m. Issuer treasury can approve $450m of payouts while retaining the required $50m buffer. The bank confirms completion. Cash closes at $50m and obligations at $1.55bn."

**1:55–2:30 — Exceptions**
"On another run, I can delay bank confirmation after fund processing. The $200m stays pending, and payout approval stays blocked. I can also repeat the same bank notice after receipt. The original reference is recognized, so cash does not increase again."

**2:30–3:00 — Compare records**
"The conventional view shows separate issuer, fund, and bank records with reconciliation. The proposed shared workflow ledger shows the same linked events to authorized participants. The bank and fund remain authoritative for their own records. Cash does not move through the ledger. This demo shows the proposed workflow and control points. It does not prove that blockchain outperforms conventional automation."
